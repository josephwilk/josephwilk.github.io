Killer Crips 3000 - 2026

Materials: Tabletop roleplaying game.

Themes: Play, Disability, Disabled Rage

Website: https://dis-ark.itch.io/kc3000

... I want disabled rage to make us fearless and unstoppable. Let's make friends with rage, lets make friends with our broken hearts. No more inwards turning anger. Let our rage meet the sun and touch eveything in between. Let our rage inherit the earth"
-- Excerpt from "I want more disabled Rage by DOLLY SEN

Killer Crips 3000 is a tabletop role-playing game of for 2-4 players, inspired by the lived experience of disabled people.

It's such an unexpected relief to be so fluidly and unashamedly shown our world as one that disabled people have always lived + struggled + done cool shit in.
Rowan, Rook and Decard

Designed around a cathartic release of disabled rage.

This game recognises the pain and loss that’s brought you to the brink and then plays with the alchemical transformation of anger into cathartic, sometimes gleeful, sometimes silly anti-oppressive violence
Rowan, Rook and Decard

Set in a fantasy world where disabled people already fought and won a war against an ableist regime but in the process something ripped open the seams of reality bringing nightmare horrors into the world.

The "Crip" in Killer Crips comes from a term that we, along with some of our community, have reclaimed through disability scholarship, culture and activism. You can read about Crip reclamation here:

https://nowthenmagazine.com/articles/crip-a-story-of-reclamation

There are prebuilt disabled characters to play as, including a cognitively simpler character with easy rules. The game avoids characters who are granted superpowers by their disability, a common trope in Hollywood fiction.

Characters special abilities are powered by Spoons, earned through describing acts of care and rest. Spoons are a reference to Spoon Theory, a way to explain energy limitations for those with chronic illnesses.

Characters get to pick one historial disabled hero's item as a piece of equipment they can use (for example Rosa Luxemburg’s Pet Cat, Ian Drury’s Microphone, Lord Nelson’s Sword). Connecting to real disabled people in history. This comes from the Disability Action Research Kollective's zine work on exploring disabled history through a non-medical lense: https://www.historyworkshop.org.uk/disability-history/politicising-disability-through-zines/
